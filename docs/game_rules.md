# La Podrida / La Pocha — Game Rules

> Compiled from several web sources (listed at the end). La Podrida has no single official rulebook, and nearly every group plays its own version. This document gives the **core rules** that all sources agree on, then the **variations** by topic, then a short summary of each source.

---

## 1. Overview

- **Type:** a trick-taking game where you must predict exactly how many tricks you will take.
- **Names:** *La Podrida* (Argentina and Latin America), *La Pocha* (Spain), *Tutecillo*, *Marrana*, *Compota*, *Las Bazas* (Mexico). It is the Hispanic version of the English game **Oh Hell!** (also called *Contract Whist* or *Blackout*).
- **Why "podrida" ("rotten"):** the name comes from spoiling ("pudrir") other players' contracts. A player who can no longer make their own bid tries to make others take too many or too few tricks.
- **Main difference from Oh Hell:** a player who cannot follow suit **must play a trump**. Many versions also require you to **beat the cards already played** when you can (*montar* / *matar*), as in Tute.
- **Goal:** take *exactly* the number of tricks you bid. Taking more or fewer counts as a miss.

---

## 2. Core rules (common to all sources)

1. **Deal.** The dealer deals a set number of cards to each player, one at a time. The number of cards changes from hand to hand (see §3.3).
2. **Trump.** The next card of the stock is turned face up, and its suit is trump for that hand. If the whole deck was dealt, the dealer's own last card sets trump.
3. **Bidding.** Starting with the player next to the dealer, each player says how many tricks they expect to take. Zero is allowed. **The dealer bids last.**
4. **Dealer restriction (the "podrida" rule).** The dealer may **not** make a bid that would bring the total of all bids to the number of tricks in the hand. So at least one player always misses.
5. **Play.** The first player (*mano*) leads any card. The others play in turn and must:
   - **follow suit** (*asistir* / *responder*) if they can;
   - otherwise, **play a trump** (*fallar*) if they can;
   - otherwise, play any card.
6. **Winning a trick.** The highest trump wins. If no trump was played, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads the next trick.
7. **Scoring.** After the hand, players who made their bid exactly get a bonus plus trick points. Players who missed get nothing, or lose points (see §3.7).
8. **End.** After a set number of hands (or when a target score is reached), the highest total wins.

## 3. Variations by topic

### 3.1 Deck

| Version | Deck | Card ranking, high → low |
|---|---|---|
| Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.) | French 52-card deck, no jokers | A K Q J 10 9 … 2 |
| GameRules.com | French 52-card deck | A K Q J 10 9 … 2 |
| Mexico ("Juego de las Bazas") | French 52-card deck, no jokers | A K Q J 10 9 … 2 |
| Spain: Ludoteka, Fournier, Spanish Wikipedia | Spanish 40-card deck (oros, copas, espadas, bastos) | **A 3** Rey Caballo Sota 7 6 5 4 2 (Tute order) |
| Spain: Stay and Play PDF | Spanish 40-card deck | A Rey Caballo Sota 7 6 5 4 **3** 2 (the 3 is *not* raised) |

Card points do not matter in any version. Only the number of tricks counts.

**Adjusting the Spanish deck to the number of players** (Spanish Wikipedia / Ludoteka):

| Players | Cards used | Most cards per player |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 36 (remove the 2s) | 12 |
| 4 | 40 (or 36 without the 2s) | 10 (or 9) |
| 5 | 40 | 8 |
| 6 | 36 (remove the 2s) | 6 |
| 7 | 35 (remove one 4), or leave the trump card out | 5 |
| 8 | 40 (Ludoteka adds 8s and 9s for 6–8 players) | 5 |

### 3.2 Number of players

- Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.): technically 2 to 13, but "worth playing" with **3 to 6**.
- GameRules / Mexico: 3–7.
- Spain: 3–6 (sometimes 8 or 10). **4 or 5 is ideal.**

### 3.3 Hand sequence (cards dealt per hand)

This is where the versions differ most. Let *N* = number of players and *Max* = the most cards each player can get.

| Version | Sequence |
|---|---|
| **Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.)** — "mano máxima" | Start at **Max**, go down 1 per hand to 5. Play **N hands of 4 cards**. Go back up from 5 to **Max**. |
| **GameRules.com** | Start at 10 cards (3–5 players), 8 (6 players) or 7 (7 players). Go down 1 per hand to **1**, then back up to the start. Example with 8: 8,7,…,2,1,2,…,7,8 = 15 hands. |
| **Spain (Ludoteka / Fournier / Stay and Play)** — the usual order | Start at **1**, go up 1 per hand to Max. Play **N hands at Max**. Go back down to 1. |
| Ludoteka, short versions | (a) Go up from 1 to Max and stop. (b) Play only hands at Max. |
| **Spanish Wikipedia** | **N hands of 1 card** (or just one, for a shorter game). Go up to Max. Play **2–3 rounds of N hands** at Max. Go down to 1, then optionally play N more 1-card hands. |
| **Mexico ("Juego de las Bazas")** | Go up from **3 to 7**. Play **N hands of 7**. Go back down to 3. |

Number of hands in the F.I.DE.PO. version (52 cards):

| Players | Hands | Max hand |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 29 | 17 |
| 4 | 22 | 13 |
| 5 | 17 | 10 |
| 6 | 14 | 8 |

(Example for 3 players: 13 hands going down from 17 to 5, then 3 hands of 4, then 13 hands going back up from 5 to 17.)

### 3.4 Dealing, direction and the first player

- **Spanish and Mexican versions:** the player on the dealer's **left cuts**. Cards go **counter-clockwise**. The player on the dealer's **right is *mano***: they bid first and lead first. The deal passes to the right (counter-clockwise).
- **Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.):** the dealer deals to the right, where the *jugador-mano* sits. The deal also passes to the right.
- **GameRules.com (Oh Hell style):** deal and play go **clockwise**. The player on the dealer's **left** bids and leads first.
- The first dealer is chosen at random (draw or any standard method).

### 3.5 Bidding (*prometer*, *pedir*, *previsiones*)

- Everyone bids in turn, and the dealer bids last. Any number from 0 to the number of cards dealt is allowed.
- **Dealer restriction:** found in every source. The forbidden number is called the ***sucias*** in Argentine slang ("¡Sucias las cuatro!" = "four is dirty", meaning the dealer can't bid 4). When the dealer can't bid the number they wanted, they are ***pudrirse*** ("getting rotten").
- **Exception:** Ludoteka drops the restriction in **1-card hands**.
- **Changing a bid:**
  - GameRules: you may change your bid until the player on your left has bid. The dealer may change theirs until the first card is played.
  - Spanish Wikipedia: once made, a bid cannot be changed.
- **Over/under:** when total bids are below the tricks available, the hand is *por defecto / de menos* (under-bid). When above, it is *por exceso / de más* (over-bid).

### 3.6 Play obligations

All versions: **follow suit if you can; if you're void, you must trump; if you have neither, play anything.** The stricter rules differ:

| Rule | Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.) | GameRules | Spain (Ludoteka / Fournier / Stay and Play / Wikipedia) | Mexico |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Must follow suit | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Must **beat** the highest card of the led suit if able (*montar*) | **No** ("no es obligación matar, pero sí responder") | Not stated | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| Void in led suit → must trump | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Must **overtrump** if a trump is already on the table | Not required | Not stated | **Yes**, if able (Ludoteka) | **Yes**, if able. If you can't, you must still play a lower trump. |
| Trump led (*arrastrar*) → others must play trump | Yes (follow suit) | Yes (follow suit) | Yes, and beat it if able | Yes |

Finer points from the sources:

- **Spanish Wikipedia:** once someone has trumped (*fallado*), the next players must still follow suit but **no longer need to beat** the led suit. A void player must trump only if they hold a trump **higher** than the one on the table. Otherwise they don't have to waste a trump.
- **Fournier / Stay and Play:** "if a player has no trump higher than the one played, they may play any card". Here overtrumping is required, but a trump that can't win doesn't have to be played.
- **Mexico:** when a third player has already trumped, a player holding several cards of the led suit may play any of them (no need to beat).
- **Renuncio** (revoke: not following suit or not trumping when required):
  - Argentina: assumed to be accidental. The cards are returned and the hand is replayed from the revoke.
  - Spanish Wikipedia: a penalty agreed in advance, usually **−50 points**.

### 3.7 Scoring

Let *b* = bid and *t* = tricks taken.

| Version | Made bid exactly (t = b) | Missed bid (t ≠ b) |
|---|---|---|
| **Argentina (F.I.DE.PO.)** | **10 + t** (1 per trick + 10 bonus) | **t** (1 per trick, no bonus) |
| **GameRules — "simplest"** | **10 + b** | 0 |
| **GameRules — "most common"** | 10 + 1 per trick (text unclear) | 1 per trick (probably) |
| **Spain (Ludoteka, Fournier, Stay and Play, Wikipedia) & Mexico** | **10 + 5·t** | **−5 · \|t − b\|** |

Examples:
- *F.I.DE.PO.:* bid 2, take 2 → 12. Bid 2, take 1 → 1. Bid 2, take 3 → 3.
- *Spain:* bid 2, take 2 → 20. Bid 0, take 0 → 10. Bid 5, take 4 or 6 → −5. Bid 1, take 4 → −15.

**Optional bonuses and multipliers (Spain):**
- **"Pocha":** with 5 or more cards dealt (Wikipedia; one app says 6 or more), bidding **all** the tricks and making them scores **100 points** (Wikipedia) or **double** (other sources/apps).
- **Suit multipliers:** hands where **copas** is trump ×2 and **oros** ×3, or only **oros** ×2. Usually all hands score the same.

**Score sheet:** one player is the scorekeeper (*planillero*). The usual sheet has a column for cards per hand and, for each player, the bid and the running total. GameRules' shorthand: cross out failed bids and write a "1" before successful ones (so a made bid of 5 reads as 15).

### 3.8 End of game and ties

- **Most versions:** the game ends after the agreed hand sequence. **Highest score wins.**
- **GameRules:** play to an agreed target score instead.
- **Ties:**
  - Ludoteka / Fournier / Stay and Play: play extra **1-card hands** until the tie breaks.
  - Spanish Wikipedia: **fewest missed bids** wins. If still tied, play an extra hand with all cards dealt, repeating as needed.

### 3.9 Special hands (Spanish Wikipedia, agreed before the game)

- **Indias:** each player holds their card on their forehead without looking. They see everyone else's card but not their own, and bid from that.
- **Subastado:** no trump card is turned. Players bid in an auction, and the highest bidder picks trump. Ties go to the player furthest to the dealer's right.
- **Mano de oro:** oros is always trump.
- **Sin triunfo:** no trump suit.
- **Chipoco** ("ni mucho ni poco"): named only, no rules given.
- **Chorizo:** a player who expects to lose the hand can drop a card on the floor, and the hand is redealt.
- **La Digui:** the stake doubles every two games.

## 4. Source-by-source summary

### 4.1 Federación Internacional de Podrida (F.I.DE.PO.) — Argentina
The most detailed Argentine source. It describes itself as "the version most widely accepted" by its group, and was designed "to remove luck as much as possible".
- 52-card French deck, 3–6 players (2–13 possible).
- Sequence: Max down to 5, then N hands of 4, then back up to Max.
- The dealer deals to the right. The *jugador-mano* is on the dealer's right. The top card of the leftover stock sets trump.
- Must follow suit, **not** required to beat. Void → must trump. Void in both → any card. If trump is led, others must follow with trump.
- The dealer can't make the bids add up to the tricks available.
- Scoring: **1 per trick + 10 for making your bid**.
- Revoke: the hand is replayed from the revoke.
- Includes a large slang glossary (see §5).

### 4.2 GameRules.com — "La Podrida"
- 52-card deck, 3–7 players, clockwise (Oh Hell style).
- Start at 10/8/7 cards, go down to 1, then back up.
- Must follow suit. Void → must trump. Void in both → any card.
- Dealer restriction. Bids may be changed until the next player bids, and the dealer may change theirs until the first card is played.
- Scoring: 10 + bid if made (or "1 per trick + 10 for exact"). Play to a target score.

### 4.3 Pagat.com — "La Podrida"
- Short entry: the Spanish equivalent of Oh Hell!. The only rule difference noted is that **a player who can't follow suit must trump**.
- Links to the F.I.DE.PO. Argentine rules and the Mexican variant.

### 4.4 Ludoteka — "La Pocha" (Spain)
- 40-card Spanish deck, 3/4/5/6/8 players (remove the 2s for 3 or 6 players).
- Sequence goes up from 1 to Max, then back down, with short versions available.
- Counter-clockwise. *Mano* is on the dealer's right.
- Must follow suit **and beat** if able. Void → must trump **and overtrump** if able.
- Dealer restriction, except in 1-card hands.
- Scoring: **+10 + 5 per trick if made, −5 per trick of difference if missed**. Ties: 1-card hands.

### 4.5 Naipes Heraclio Fournier — "Pocha" (Spain)
- Same as Ludoteka: 40 cards, ranking A-3-R-C-S-7-6-5-4-2, remove the 2s for 3 players.
- *Montar* (beat) or *fallar* (trump) when possible. If trump is led, everyone holding trump must play it.
- Scoring: 10 + 5 per trick / −5 per trick missed. Ties: 1-card hands.

### 4.6 Stay and Play Cards — "La Pocha" (PDF)
- Same structure as Fournier. The ranking does **not** raise the 3 (A-R-C-S-7-6-5-4-3-2).
- Usual sequence: 1 → Max, then N hands at Max, then Max → 1.
- Scoring: 10 + 5 per trick / −5 per trick missed. Ties: 1-card hands.

### 4.7 Spanish Wikipedia — "Podrida (juego de naipes)"
- 40-card Spanish deck. Includes a table for adjusting the deck to 3–8 players.
- Most detailed obligations: once someone has trumped, others need not beat; overtrump only if you can win.
- Bids are final once made.
- Scoring 10 + 5·t / −5 per trick missed. **Pocha = 100**. Suit multipliers (copas ×2, oros ×3). Revoke −50.
- Special hands: Indias, Subastado, Mano de oro, Sin triunfo, Chorizo, etc. Ties: fewest missed bids.

### 4.8 Gonzalo Mena-Mendoza — "Juego de las Bazas" (Mexico)
- Adapted from Ludoteka's rules for the **52-card French deck**, 3–7 players.
- Sequence: 3 → 7, then N hands of 7, then 7 → 3.
- Must beat in the led suit and overtrump if able. If you can't overtrump, you must still play a trump.
- Scoring: 10 + 5 per trick / −5 per trick missed.

## 5. Glossary (*vocabulario podridero*)

Mostly from F.I.DE.PO. (Argentina), with additions from Spanish sources.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mano** / jugador-mano | The player who bids first and leads the first trick. |
| **Dador** | Dealer. |
| **Planillero** | Scorekeeper. |
| **Prometer / pedir** | To bid (declare your contract). |
| **Cumplir** | To make exactly your bid. |
| **Sucias** | The number the dealer can't bid. |
| **Pudrirse** | Being unable to bid what you wanted because of the dealer restriction. |
| **Podrido** | A player who deliberately tries to make others miss, usually after their own bid is already lost. |
| **Mano podrida** | A hand where nobody made their bid. |
| **Mano vidriosa** | A tricky hand where it's hard to decide on a bid. |
| **Por defecto / de menos** | Total bids are lower than the tricks available. |
| **Por exceso / de más** | Total bids are higher than the tricks available. |
| **A menos** | A zero bid, or the strategy of taking no tricks. |
| **Responder / asistir** | To follow the suit led. |
| **Montar / matar** | To beat the cards already played. |
| **Fallar** | To play a trump because you're void in the suit led. |
| **Arrastrar** | (Spain) To lead trump, forcing others to play trump. (Argentina) To win a run of tricks, especially with trumps. |
| **Saltar** | To be forced to win a trick you didn't want. |
| **Tragar / caerse la cara / presente griego** | To take an unexpected trick. |
| **Blanco** | A player with no trumps left. |
| **Cargas** | Cards you need to get rid of. |
| **Freno** | A low card used to lead once your contract is already made. |
| **Ir a ver** | To lead a low non-trump card. |
| **La "tamótica"** / **La bestial** | Leading a low trump / a very high trump. |
| **Quemar caucho** | Leading a trump, especially early in the hand. |
| **Realizar** | (As *mano*) cashing your aces and kings before others run out of the suit. |
| **Revisar** | Leading a suit repeatedly until others run out of it and have to trump. |
| **Palo corto / largo / faltante** | A suit you hold few / many / no cards of. |
| **Renuncio** | Revoke: failing to follow suit or to trump when required. |
| **Pocha** | (Spain) Bidding and making all the tricks. |
| **Rock de la hiena** | Running complaints from a losing player. |
